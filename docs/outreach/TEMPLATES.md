# Claima outreach templates

Rules that apply to every template: no em or en dashes, no ranges, no dollar figure
that is not either CMS-published for that specific org or the $65,000 loaded biller
cost. Always keep the leak-report CTA and the opt-out line. Apollo appends the
signature, so never sign off in the body.

---

## A. Buyer, practice with CMS-verifiable Medicare volume
Target: revenue cycle director, billing manager, practice administrator, COO, CFO.
Only the CMS figure is quoted; nothing derived.

```
Hey {first},

Saw you're hiring billing staff. A biller runs about $65,000 loaded once you
count benefits.

CMS shows {practice} billed {medicare_total} in Medicare last year, and that's
just the Medicare book. Most of the books I go through have aged balances nobody
ever worked. Not bad claims, just accounts nobody had time for.

You don't have to replace your billers. Claima's AI works the aged accounts your
team never reaches.

Your own aging export gives you the number in two minutes: claima.io/leak-report.
Browser only, nothing uploaded.

Not relevant? Reply "no thanks" and I won't write again. Claima LLC, New York, NY.
```

## C. Billing company or RCM firm
Their labour IS the product, so never pitch headcount savings. Pitch margin, and
let them self-select between licensing and handing us the work.

```
Hey {first},

At Claima we built an AI RCM tool that works claims and aged A/R. It reads a
100,000 line aging report in under a second.

Most of the books I go through have aged balances nobody ever worked. Not bad
claims, just accounts that never came up the queue.

Either way works, whichever you prefer. License it and {crew} works what it flags,
or hand us the aged book and we handle the claims and split what comes in.

Try it on a client file: claima.io/leak-report. Browser only, nothing uploaded.

Not relevant? Reply "no thanks" and I won't write again. Claima LLC, New York, NY.
```

## D. ⭐ NOT the buyer, ask for a warm intro
Use for anyone at a target firm who does not own A/R: physicians, office managers,
schedulers, IT, HR, front desk leads, marketing. This is NOT a pitch. It asks one
question, because an intro-ask converts on brevity. Do not add the product story.

```
Hey {first},

You're probably not the right person, but you'd know who is.

Who at {org} owns aged A/R and denials? We built an AI tool that works the
accounts nobody gets to, and I'd rather ask than guess.

One name is all I need, and I'll take it from there.

Not relevant? Reply "no thanks" and I won't write again. Claima LLC, New York, NY.
```

## Multi-contact rule
Three to five contacts per firm. Who gets which template depends on whether they
could plausibly buy, not on seniority.

**Plausible buyers, each gets a pitch (A, B or C):**
| Role | Why they buy |
|---|---|
| Revenue cycle director, billing manager | Feels the aged A/R daily. The champion |
| CEO, COO, owner | Signs |
| CFO | Approves the budget |

**Not a buyer, gets template D, the intro-ask:** physicians, office managers,
schedulers, front desk leads, IT, HR, marketing, credentialing. They cannot buy
this, so pitching them wastes the touch. Ask them for one name instead.

Earlier version of this rule said only ONE person per firm may get a pitch. That
was wrong: all three roles above are real buyers at a practice or billing firm, and
reaching all of them is the point of multi-threading. The line that matters is
buyer vs non-buyer, not first vs second contact.
