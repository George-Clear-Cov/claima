## What the Apollo pull actually gave us — and its limits

272 people matched. Three caveats that matter before anyone sends an email:

1. **A large share are Investor Relations.** "Partner, Head of Investor Relations",
   "VP, Fundraising & IR" — these people raise money *from LPs*. They are not who takes
   a founder pitch. Filtered out above; do not confuse the two.
2. **Apollo obfuscates last names** (`Ma***a`) until a record is enriched, which spends
   lead credits. 2,332 available, cycle resets 2026-09-07.
3. **Apollo does not know investment focus.** It has titles, not "leads seed-stage
   healthcare RCM." Every one of these firms publishes its partner roster with sector
   focus on its own site — for VC targeting that is better data than Apollo, and free.

# How to attack this list

## The governing constraint: each partner is a one-shot resource

70 emails is not 70 attempts. It is 70 *single* attempts. A partner who reads
"pre-revenue, solo, still employed at AmEx" forms a view and files you. Coming back in
six months with real numbers is materially harder than arriving fresh — you are now the
person who pitched too early. **Sending today spends the list at its lowest possible
conversion.**

## Two unlocks, in order

**1. The Farag BAA.** Converts the traction answer from "one pilot in contracting" to
"live pilot, $96,952 book, 31-52% recoverable, $8.9-15.0k to us." That is the difference
between a story and a business. It costs one signature.

**2. The full-time answer.** Institutional pre-seed investors fund full-time founders.
"I'm still at AmEx" ends most conversations in minute five, and hedging reads worse than
either honest answer. Decide it before the first meeting, not during one. If the answer
is "on a term sheet," say exactly that — it is a real answer and it lands.

Until both are settled, use the list for *research*, not outreach.

## Then: warm before cold

Cold email into a Tier-1 fund converts near zero. Before sending anything:
- Run all 70 names through LinkedIn for 1st/2nd-degree connections.
- Mine the AmEx network — payments/banking people overlap heavily with fintech VC.
- A single warm intro to Oak HC/FT outperforms 70 cold sends.

## Then: sequence worst-fit → best-fit

Counterintuitive but right. **Pitch the funds you care least about first**, to debug the
pitch on live partners. Your framing, your numbers, and your answers to the obvious
objections will all be sharper by the fifth conversation than the first.

| Wave | Firms | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — practice | Greycroft, FirstMark, First Round, Lux | Real meetings, low cost of a no. Learn which objection recurs. |
| 2 — realistic | **Primary**, Venrock, Bessemer healthcare | NYC seed and healthcare specialists. This is where a check most plausibly comes from. |
| 3 — the ones that matter | **Oak HC/FT**, **F-Prime**, **BCV**, **General Catalyst** | Only after the pitch survives waves 1-2. |

## The uncomfortable inversion

**The ranking runs opposite to your odds.** Sequoia is #1 and will never do this deal.
Primary Venture Partners is #85 — NYC, seed-stage, fintech-literate — and might. Treat
the Stanford score as a measure of *fund quality*, never of *fit*. The realistic yes on
this list is in Tier 3 of the ranking, not Tier 1.

## Cold email shape, when you get there

Four sentences. No deck attached, no calendar link in the first mail.
1. One line of credibility that is specific to them (their portfolio company, not flattery).
2. The finding, in numbers: 78 accounts, $96,952, **68% never worked by anyone.**
3. What Claima is, in one sentence.
4. One ask: 20 minutes.

The UnitedHealthcare-under-five-names detail ($15,399, their #2 payer, invisible) is the
strongest single thing you have. It proves you have touched real data, which is what
separates you from every deck-only founder in their inbox.
